ICC Profiles Descriptions

All the HiColor look profiles involve proprietary Kodak color science and color management technology. The color moves incorporated into these profiles have been specially designed and tuned for the Kodak Professional DCS images. The colors are more saturated in general, but they also are more normalized. This means that colors that started out very saturated with the standard looks are not moved as much as colors that were less saturated. This provides a better overall balanced look to the image, and prevents some colors from being too saturated. In addition, colors near the edge of the color gamut are “soft clipped” to prevent hard clipping at either 0 or 255. Tuning of these looks was performed in ProPhotoRGB color space, and this is the recommended working space for viewing the images in Adobe© PhotoshopTM.

KodakDCSProductHiColorHold.icm and DCSProductHiColorHold.icm

These look profiles have the standard “product” tone scale*. However, bright colors are more highly saturated except for skin related colors. Skin colors are “held” in place and other colors are boosted. Note that any color in the image that is the same hue as skin will also not be affected. Additional characteristics are that lightly saturated colors are not affected, and colors that were highly saturated to begin with are not affected as much as medium saturated colors.

These looks would be very useful for commercial images that involve both people and other colored objects (e.g. clothing). After applying these looks, the people’s skin tone would be unaffected, but other colors would become more saturated.

KodakDCSProductHiColor.icm and DCSProductHiColor.icm

These look profiles have the standard “product” tone scale. Bright colors are more highly saturated. Skin colors are not “held” in place and are affected. Additional characteristics are that lightly saturated colors are not affected, and colors that were highly saturated to begin with are not affected as much as medium saturated colors.

These looks would be generally useful for commercial images. After applying these looks all bright colors would become more saturated.

KodakDCSPortraitHiColorHold.icm and DCSPortraitHiColorHold.icm

These look profiles have the standard “portrait” tone scale. However, bright colors are more highly saturated except for skin related colors. Skin colors are “held” in place and other colors are boosted. Note that any color in the image that is the same hue as skin will also not be affected. Additional characteristics are that lightly saturated colors are not affected, and colors that were highly saturated to begin with are not affected as much as medium saturated colors.

These looks would be very useful for portrait images that not only involve the people, but also have other bright colors of interest. After applying this look, the people’s skin tone would be unaffected, but other colors would become more saturated.

KodakDCSPortraitHiColor.icm and DCSPortraitHiColor.icm

These look profiles have the standard “portrait” tone scale. Bright colors are more highly saturated. Skin colors are not “held” in place and are affected. Additional characteristics are that lightly saturated colors are not affected, and colors that were highly saturated to begin with are not affected as much as medium saturated colors.

These looks would be generally useful for portrait images where the skin tones of the people and other scene objects need more color saturation. If the skin tones become too saturated, use the color saturation control in Kodak Professional DCS Capture Studio to reduce the color to an acceptable level.

KodakDCSHiContrast.icm and DCSHiContrast.icm

These look profiles have a higher contrast tone scale compared to the standard “product” look*. The color saturation has been controlled so that saturated colors do not hard clip with the contrast increase.

These looks are useful in general for images that need more contrast with control of saturated colors.

KodakDCSB&WNormal.icm and DCSB&WNormal.icm

These look profiles provide a standard black and white rendition of the original color image. However the grayscale has been modeled using the Helmholtz-Kohlrausch effect whereby the perceived brightness increases with increased color saturation. This is a quite different look than adjusting the color saturation tool in Kodak Professional DCS Capture Studio to render a black and white image.

KodakDCSB&WWratten8.icm and DCSB&WWratten8.icm

These look profiles provide a black and white rendition of the color image with a simulated Wratten #8 (yellow) filter. These looks attempt to be equivalent to using black and white film with the Wratten #8 filter over the lens. The Wratten #8 filter is useful for darkening blue sky in landscape images, and lightening skin tones in portraits.

KodakDCSB&WWratten25.icm and DCSB&WWratten8.icm

These look profiles provide a black and white rendition of the color image with a simulated Wratten #25 (red) filter. These looks attempt to be equivalent to using black and white film with the Wratten #25 filter over the lens. The Wratten #25 filter is useful for dramatically darkening blue sky in landscape images, and other special affects.

KodakDCSSepia1.icm, KodakDCSSepia2.icm, DCSSepia1.icm, and DCSSepia2.icm

These look profiles provide a classic sepia colored tone to the image. These looks attempt to be equivalent to a black and white print which was sepia toned. The difference between Sepia1 and Sepia2 is the hue of the toning effect. Sepia1 is “warmer” than Sepia2. Note that the strength of the sepia tone can be adjusted with the color saturation control in Kodak Professional DCS Capture Studio.

*Note: The Kodak Professional Software products that can use the DCS Custom Look ICC Profiles install two looks named “product” and “portrait” (the default look is product). The product look is good for general commercial images that involve people and objects. The portrait look has a lower contrast and is good for general portraiture of people.
